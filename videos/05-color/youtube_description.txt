TITLE:
The Color You Were Told to Wear: How Pink Became a "Girl" Color

DESCRIPTION:
In 1918, a trade magazine told baby stores: pink is for boys, blue is for girls. So who flipped it — and who decided a newborn needs a color at all?

For most of the 1800s every small child wore white. Then stores started guessing, department stores disagreed for decades, and by the 1950s the guesses hardened into a "rule". After a brief unisex break in the '70s, prenatal tests in the '80s gave the industry something new to sell: a specific kind of baby, months before birth. Pink aisles, the pink tax, gender reveal parties — and why the rule still teaches itself.
