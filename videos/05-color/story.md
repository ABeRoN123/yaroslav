# EP05 — The Color You Were Told to Wear (pink for girls, blue for boys)

## Verified outline (hedge where marked)
1. Cold open: "In 1918, a trade magazine told parents pink was for boys."
2. Victorian babies: all children in white dresses; boys "breeched" (got trousers) at ~6–7. White = bleachable, practical.
3. Mid-1800s: pastels arrive, pink and blue — but no gender rule yet.
4. June 1918, trade paper *Infant's Department*: "The generally accepted rule is pink for the boy and blue for the girl." Reason: pink = stronger, more decided; blue = delicate, dainty.
5. 1927: *Time* printed what big department stores advised — Filene's (Boston), Best & Co (NY), Halle Bros (Cleveland), Marshall Field's (Chicago) said pink for boys; others the opposite.
6. Mid-20th century: the modern rule settles. HEDGE: Del Giudice (2012) newspaper data 1881–1930: 34 standard vs 28 reversed.
7. 1960s–70s: unisex wave; Sears catalog reportedly had no pink toddler clothes for two years in the 1970s.
8. ~1985: prenatal testing → shop before birth → gendered everything. Paoletti: "The more you individualize clothing, the more you can sell."
9. Why it still works: hand-me-downs blocked → second kid = second set. Pink tax (NYC DCA 2015: ~800 products, women's ~7% more). Gender reveal party: 2008 blog post (cake).
10. Callback ending → "Made to Want."
