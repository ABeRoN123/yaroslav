Before 1915, almost no woman in America shaved her armpits. Then a woman in a magazine raised her arm.

In 1915 a Harper's Bazaar ad told women that "Summer Dress and Modern Dancing" made it "necessary" to remove "objectionable hair." The same year Gillette launched the Milady Décolleté, the first razor sold to women — basically the men's razor in a prettier case. Then came X-ray hair removal, a rat-poison cream called Koremlu, wartime nylon shortages and painted-on stockings. A hundred years later, the habit is still here.
